Lull

Breathe a little slower and deeper, on the inhale and the exhale, in whatever way feels comfortable.

If you need a sigh, a yawn, or a different kind of breath at any point, take it. There is no right pace.

Comfort first

Ready when you are

Time min

For your comfort

  • Sit or lie in any position that feels good.
  • Slow and deep means easy, not big. Never strain or force a breath.
  • If you feel lightheaded or uneasy, stop and breathe normally.

Research-backed ways to settle stress. Try one that appeals, and skip anything that doesn’t feel comfortable.

Cyclic sighing1 to 5 minutes
  1. Breathe in through your nose, then add a second, smaller sip of air on top.
  2. Let a long, unhurried breath out through your mouth.
  3. Repeat for a few minutes, at whatever pace is easy.

In a 2023 Stanford trial, a few minutes of daily cyclic sighing improved mood more than mindfulness meditation.

Progressive muscle relaxation5 to 15 minutes
  1. Start at your feet. Tense the muscles gently for about five seconds.
  2. Release fully and notice the difference for a few breaths.
  3. Work upward: calves, thighs, belly, hands, arms, shoulders, face.

Tense gently, and skip any area that is sore or injured. A long line of research links it to lower anxiety and better sleep.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding1 to 2 minutes
  1. Name 5 things you can see.
  2. 4 things you can feel, 3 you can hear.
  3. 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.

It pulls attention out of racing thoughts and into the present moment, a common tool in anxiety care.

Body scan5 to 20 minutes

Lying or sitting, move your attention slowly from your head to your toes. Notice each area without trying to change it.

Mindfulness-based programs reliably reduce stress and rumination in controlled studies.

Name the feeling1 minute

Say or write what you feel in a few words, such as “I feel anxious about tomorrow’s meeting.”

Putting feelings into words (affect labeling) has been shown to calm the brain’s alarm response.

Time in nature20 minutes

Spend time in a park, garden, or any green space. Leave your phone in your pocket and just look around.

One study found about 20 minutes outdoors was enough to lower the stress hormone cortisol.

These are general wellness ideas, not medical advice. If stress feels constant or overwhelming, talking with a doctor or therapist can help.
